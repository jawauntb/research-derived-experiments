# Ecological Compiler Source and Claim Ledger

**Research date:** 2026-08-26

**Purpose:** Preserve the evidence boundary behind the final essay. This is a
curated synthesis, not a full-text source archive.

## Claim decisions

| Claim | Evidence class | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Low ultraviolet environments changed the adaptive problem for human pigmentation and vitamin-D acquisition. | Strong review and population-genetic evidence. | Retain, with cultural and dietary substitution made explicit. |
| EPA or DHA is a biochemical precursor of dopamine. | Contradicted by established catecholamine biochemistry. | Reject. Dopamine is synthesized from tyrosine through L-DOPA. |
| Omega-3 status can affect neural membranes and dopaminergic systems. | Mechanistically plausible; strongest evidence comes from cell and animal work. | Retain as bounded modulation, not a population-level cognitive scalar. |
| More marine omega-3 generally improves adult cognition. | Randomized human evidence is small, heterogeneous, and mostly null for global cognition. | Reject as a general enhancement claim; retain deficiency-relief possibility. |
| Fishing dependence robustly predicts jurisdictional hierarchy across societies. | Direct preregistered Study I result. | Reject. Adjusted point estimate is positive, but ancestry- and space-sensitive intervals include zero, the sign reverses after three large language families are removed, and the registered social-variable attenuation pattern fails. |
| Aquatic resources can shape political organization through clumping, storage, defensibility, and ownership. | Strong regional comparative evidence; plausible cultural-evolution mechanism. | Retain as context-specific resource topology, not a fish molecule effect. |
| Maritime connectivity changes trade and development. | Strong historical and quasi-experimental evidence. | Retain, with institutional heterogeneity explicit. |
| Ecology alone explains European state formation or inherited cognitive superiority. | Unsupported and contradicted by comparative cases. | Reject. |
| Durable capability requires a carrier that stores, transmits, selects, and recombines knowledge. | Conceptual synthesis consistent with distributed cognition, cultural evolution, and institutional evidence. | Retain as the ecological-compiler research program, not as a proven scalar law. |

## Primary empirical sources

1. Kirby, K. R., et al. 2016. “D-PLACE: A Global Database of Cultural,
   Linguistic and Environmental Diversity.” *PLOS ONE* 11(7): e0158391.
   <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0158391>. D-PLACE joins cultural,
   environmental, geographic, and language information while warning about
   spatial and historical dependence.
2. D-PLACE Ethnographic Atlas contribution, version 3.2.1. 2025.
   <https://zenodo.org/records/17602181>. Study I pins source commit
   `5aa46eea62815daa283ac67cc757065a1b3be16e` and the support-data commit
   `9bfed2c8c206be00f55f71516f262bbca2234e5a`.
3. Ahedo, V., D. Zurro, J. Caro, and J. M. Galán. 2021. “Let’s Go Fishing.”
   *PLOS ONE* 16(8): e0254539.
   <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0254539>. Fishing appears throughout
   viable mixed subsistence economies, but the authors do not claim that fish
   mechanically produces state complexity.
4. Smith, E. A., and B. F. Codding. 2021. “Ecological Variation and
   Institutionalized Inequality in Hunter-Gatherer Societies.” *PNAS* 118(13):
   e2016134118. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2016134118>. Among 89 Pacific
   Coast societies, reliance on clumped aquatic resources and ownership of
   fishing sites predict hierarchy while historical and spatial dependence are
   modeled.
5. Gershman, B., and T. Mumladze. 2025. “Common Ancestry, Uncommon Findings:
   Revisiting Cross-Cultural Research in Economics.” Working paper.
   <https://www.borisgershman.com/research/phyloatlas/PhyloAtlas_Sep25.pdf>.
   Simulations and reanalyses show that family fixed effects or clustered
   uncertainty do not fully solve phylogenetic non-independence.
6. Bromham, L., et al. 2019. “Parasites and Politics: Why Cross-Cultural
   Studies Must Control for Relatedness, Proximity and Covariation.” *Royal
   Society Open Science* 6:181100. <https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.181100>.

    by duration and dose.

## Historical and archaeological sources

13. Richards, M. P., R. J. Schulting, and R. E. M. Hedges. 2003. “Sharp Shift
    in Diet at Onset of Neolithic.” *Nature* 425:366.
    <https://doi.org/10.1038/425366a>. British coastal and inland samples show
    a rapid change from marine to terrestrial diets at the Neolithic transition.
14. Pascali, L. 2017. “The Wind of Change: Maritime Technology, Trade, and
    Economic Development.” *American Economic Review* 107(9): 2821–2854.
    <https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20140832>. Steam altered effective shipping
    distances asymmetrically; trade and development effects depended on
    institutional conditions.
15. Acemoglu, D., S. Johnson, and J. A. Robinson. 2005. “The Rise of Europe:
    Atlantic Trade, Institutional Change, and Economic Growth.” *American
    Economic Review* 95(3): 546–579.
    <https://doi.org/10.1257/0002828054201305>. Atlantic trade interacted with
    preexisting constraints on monarchy and merchant political power.
16. Currie, T. E., and R. Mace. 2009. “Political Complexity Predicts the
    Spread of Ethnolinguistic Groups.” *PNAS* 106(18): 7339–7344.
    <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0804698106>. Political complexity is itself
    related to language-area expansion, making ancestry and sample composition
    part of the outcome process rather than mere nuisance variables.
