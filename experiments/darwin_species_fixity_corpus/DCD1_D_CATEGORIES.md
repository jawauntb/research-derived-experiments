## The three categories

> - **D1** — **species fixity**: the commitment that species are
>   permanent categories with essential boundaries, immutable across
>   generations, natural kinds rather than fluid populations.
>   *Examples of discussing D1:* asserting or denying that species
>   change over time; defining species boundaries as fixed or fluid;
>   arguing about whether varieties can cross species lines;
>   examining whether transmutation is possible.
>
> - **D2** — **separate creation**: the commitment that each species
>   was independently created (or independently came into being) as a
>   discrete act, rather than descending from common ancestors.
>   *Examples of discussing D2:* asserting or denying independent
>   origin of species; discussing the plurality of creations vs
>   common descent; arguing about the relation between related species
>   as separate acts vs shared ancestry.
>
> - **D3** — **species essence / archetype**: the commitment that
>   species are defined by a fixed archetypal form (Platonic essence,
>   ideal type), rather than by statistical properties of a population.
>   *Examples of discussing D3:* defining species by essential
>   characters; discussing the type specimen as fixing species
>   identity; arguing against nominal or fluid species definitions.
>
> - **OTHER** — every commitment that fits none of the above:
>   methodology, geology, demography, natural theology (when it
>   appears), mechanisms of generation, ecology, physiology, etc.
